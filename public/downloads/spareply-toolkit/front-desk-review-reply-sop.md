> Owner: [Practice Manager Name] · Last reviewed: [Date] · Clinic: [Practice Name]

This SOP is the working version of the cadence on /toolkit. Copy it into your

---

## Purpose

Reply to every Google review for [Practice Name] within 24 business hours,
without confirming protected health information in public, and without burning
weekend hours on it. Five people max should ever touch this workflow:

- Front-desk lead — drafts replies, runs the safety check, posts approved replies.
- Practice manager — approves 4–5 star drafts, co-drafts every 1–2 star reply.
- Owner — joins the Friday huddle, reads the monthly roll-up.
- Provider on call — reviewed for any clinical concern raised in a review.
- Office manager — handles billing or scheduling escalations.

## HIPAA-aware reply rules

SpaReply is HIPAA-aware editorial guidance, not legal, privacy, or compliance
advice. Have your privacy officer or counsel review your clinic's policy
before publishing replies. The rules below are the editorial guardrails the
template bank was written against.

### Do

- Thank the guest by first name only.
- Acknowledge the visit without naming the treatment unless the guest named it first.
- Praise the team member by first name if the guest named them.
- Invite further conversation through a private channel for anything sensitive.
- Keep replies under 60 words for 5-star and under 90 words for negative reviews.
- Have a second person read every reply before it goes live.

### Don't

- Confirm or deny that someone is or was a patient at [Practice Name].
- Name a specific treatment the guest didn't already mention publicly.
- Reference dosages, units, brands, products, or clinical outcomes.
- Apologize in a way that admits liability before the practice manager reviews.
- Promise refunds, comps, or remedies in public — move those offline.
- Use exclamation points, emoji, or all-caps. The clinical tone is the point.

## The 7-question pre-post safety check

Run this on every reply before it goes live. If any answer is "no," send the
draft back to the practice manager.

1. Did the guest write the detail publicly first?
2. Is the reply free of treatment specifics the guest did not name?
3. Free of outcome promises (skin cleared, pain gone, results lasted)?
4. Was anything sensitive moved to a private channel?
5. Has a second person at [Practice Name] read this?
6. Does the tone match a calm, well-run clinic?
7. Are we within 24 business hours of the review going live?

## Operating cadence

| When | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| Daily · 10 min | Front-desk lead | Pull yesterday's new reviews. Draft replies for 4- and 5-star into the shared doc. Flag anything 3 stars or below for the practice manager — do not draft those publicly until cleared. |
| Daily · 15 min | Practice manager | Approve the 4–5 star drafts, post them, log to tracker. Personally draft (or co-draft) every 1–2 star reply with the front-desk lead. |
| Tuesday · 20 min | Front-desk lead | Run the weekly SOP: triage, draft, safety check, post, log, scan for one signal to bring to Friday. |
| Friday · 10 min huddle | Owner / practice manager / front-desk lead | Share one repeating compliment and one repeating concern from this week's reviews. Decide one operational tweak. Don't try to fix everything. |
| Monthly · 30 min | Practice manager | Roll up the tracker: count by star rating, category, provider. One page, plain language. Share with owner and providers. |
| Quarterly · 60 min | Owner | Re-read the rules, the negative-review playbook, and the templates. Refresh placeholders for any new providers, services, or pricing. |

## Internal review tracker — column spec

Use the matching CSV in this folder. Columns:

- Date · Guest first name · Star rating · Category · Provider · Public reply (link) · Private follow-up status · Resolution

Rules:

- One row per review. Never enter PHI.
- "Category" values: clinical, operational, billing, hostile, praise.
- "Private follow-up status": none / sent / replied / resolved.
- "Resolution" is plain language, one line. The tracker is for signal at the monthly roll-up — not a clinical record.

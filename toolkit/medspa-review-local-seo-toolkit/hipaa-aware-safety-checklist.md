# HIPAA-Aware Safety Checklist

A 60-second pre-flight check before any public review reply, GBP post, or
social caption goes out. Designed for med spa front-desk and marketing leads.

> **This is not legal or HIPAA-compliance advice.** It is a practical reminder
> list. Final compliance decisions belong with your provider, privacy officer,
> and counsel. State laws and federal regulations change — recheck periodically.

---

## The 7-question pre-post check

Before you click "Reply" or "Post," scan this list. If you can't answer
"yes" to all seven, do not post.

1. **Did the guest write the detail publicly first?** If they did not name
   the treatment, the medication, the diagnosis, or the provider — neither
   do we.
2. **Is the reply free of any treatment specifics?** No syringe counts, no
   units, no dosages, no anatomical specifics, no "we used X on Y."
3. **Is it free of outcome promises?** "We're glad you loved it" is fine.
   "You'll see even better results next time" is not.
4. **Is anything that requires privacy moved to a private channel?** Refund
   conversations, clinical concerns, identity verification, and complaint
   resolution all happen at `[practice manager email]`, not in the reply box.
5. **Has a second person read it?** For 1- and 2-star reviews, this is
   non-negotiable.
6. **Does the tone match a calm, well-run clinic?** No emoji walls, no
   sarcasm, no defensiveness, no all-caps.
7. **Are we within 24 business hours of the review (5★) or 48 (everything
   else)?** Speed signals operational health.

## The "never write publicly" list

These belong nowhere on a public review reply, GBP post, social caption,
or any other guest-visible surface — even if the guest already wrote them:

- Names of medications, brands, syringe counts, units, or dosages
- Names of conditions, diagnoses, or skin / health concerns
- Treatment plans, schedules, or sequencing
- Photographs of the guest or any identifiable detail (initials + room
  number is enough to identify, in some cases)
- Provider names you weren't given permission to use
- Confirmation of whether someone is or has been a patient — even saying
  "we're sorry your treatment didn't work" can confirm care
- Pricing, billing, or refund details specific to one guest
- Internal incident notes, even paraphrased

When the guest has *already* posted protected health information about
themselves, your job is to acknowledge their experience without
*confirming* or *adding to* it.

## Phrases to use when in doubt

These are safe defaults that acknowledge without confirming:

- "Thank you for sharing this with us."
- "We appreciate the time you took to write."
- "We'll share your note with the team."
- "Out of respect for privacy, we won't discuss visit specifics publicly."
- "Please reach our practice manager at [practice manager email]."

---

## Phrases to avoid (with safer rewrites)

| Risky phrasing | Why it's risky | Safer rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Glad your Botox results lasted!" | Confirms treatment | "Glad to hear from you. We appreciate your trust." |
| "We'll get those results next time." | Outcome promise | "We look forward to your next visit." |
| "Your provider [name] said you tolerated it well." | Confirms care + adds detail | "Thank you for the kind note about our team." |
| "We're sorry the filler migrated." | Confirms treatment + adverse event | "We're sorry your visit didn't feel as it should — please reach our practice manager privately." |
| "We refunded $400 for your peel." | Pricing + treatment | "Please reach our practice manager so we can follow up privately." |
| "Hope your skin clears up soon!" | Implies a condition | "We're glad you visited — see you next time." |

## Photos, screenshots, and video

- Never repost a guest's before/after photo on the clinic's account, even
  if the guest posted it themselves, unless you have a signed release that
  specifically authorizes that surface (Google, Instagram, web, ads).
- Never screenshot a public review and re-post it without removing
  identifying detail — even visible first names + visit context can
  re-identify a guest.
- Treat clinic walk-through video the same way: blur or avoid filming
  rooms while a guest is on premises.

## Team training — quick role-play prompts

Use these in a 10-minute team huddle:

1. *"A guest posts a 5★ review naming the Botox dosage. Draft a public reply."*
   The right answer is: thank them, do not confirm the dosage, invite them
   back. Read it aloud and have the team flag any line that confirms care.
2. *"A 1★ review accuses a provider of pushing unnecessary treatments.
   What goes public, what goes private?"* Public: A4 script. Private: same-day
   call from the practice manager, owner looped in.
3. *"A guest emails a private complaint about a result. They later post a
   3★ review summarizing the same complaint. Do we mention the email?"*
   No. Do not reference private correspondence in a public reply.

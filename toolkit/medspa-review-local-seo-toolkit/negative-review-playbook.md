# Negative Review Playbook (1–2★)

A negative review is not a fire drill — it is a process. This playbook gives
your team the public language and the private follow-up SOP for 1- and 2-star
reviews so they stop being owner-only emergencies.

> **Reminder:** This is not legal, medical, or HIPAA-compliance advice. Sensitive
> clinical or legal questions go to your provider, privacy officer, and counsel.

---

## The 5-step cadence

Use this order. Do not skip steps.

1. **Triage within 4 business hours.** A team member acknowledges internally
   in Slack / WhatsApp / email — even if the public reply takes longer.
2. **Pull the record privately.** Look up the guest, visit date, provider,
   and any internal notes. Do not paste these into the public reply field.
3. **Write the public reply** using one of the templates in section A below.
   Keep it under 90 words.
4. **Have it reviewed.** A second pair of eyes (owner, practice manager,
   or designated lead) approves before posting. Never post a 1★ reply that
   only one person has read.
5. **Move to private.** Send the private follow-up email or call within
   1 business day using the script in section B. Log it.

---

## Section A — Public reply scripts

These scripts are deliberately short. The public reply's job is to
de-escalate, signal calm professionalism, and route to a private channel.
It is not the place to "win" the dispute.

### A1. The default 1★ public reply (use when in doubt)

> We're sorry your visit didn't feel as seamless as it should have,
> [guest first name]. We appreciate you bringing this to our attention.
> Please contact our practice manager at [practice manager email] so we
> can listen, review the details, and follow up offline with care.

### A2. 2★ where the guest flagged a specific operational issue

> Thank you for the honest feedback, [guest first name]. We hear you on
> [the specific operational issue they named]. We've shared your note with
> the team responsible and our practice manager would like to follow up
> privately at [practice manager email].

### A3. The reviewer named a clinical concern publicly

> Thank you for raising this, [guest first name]. Out of respect for privacy,
> we won't discuss medical details publicly. Please reach our practice
> manager at [practice manager email] right away — we'd like to listen and
> follow up with the appropriate provider promptly.

### A4. Strong / hostile language

> We hear you, [guest first name], and we take this seriously. We will not
> discuss visit specifics publicly out of respect for privacy, but our
> practice manager would like to listen. Please email [practice manager email]
> at your convenience.

### A5. Possible mistaken identity / non-guest

> Thank you for taking the time to write. We weren't able to find a visit
> matching this review on our end. If you've visited [clinic name], please
> email our practice manager at [practice manager email] so we can review
> the details together.

### A6. Refund / billing dispute aired publicly

> Thank you for raising this, [guest first name]. Billing questions deserve
> a careful, private conversation — please reach our practice manager at
> [practice manager email] and we'll review the visit details together.

## Section B — Private follow-up SOP

The public reply is half the job. The other half happens off-platform,
where the actual relationship gets repaired.

### B1. Within 1 business day — the first private message

Email or call. Default to email if you don't already have a phone
relationship with the guest. Keep it human.

> Subject: Following up after your recent visit
>
> Hi [guest first name],
>
> Thank you for taking the time to share your experience publicly. I'm
> [name], the practice manager at [clinic name]. I'd like to listen to
> what happened in more detail — without writing anything you'd rather
> keep private.
>
> Would a 10-minute call this week work, or is email easier? I'll also
> review the visit notes with the team before we talk so we don't waste
> your time.
>
> Thank you again for your honesty,
> [name]
> [practice manager email] · [practice manager direct line]

### B2. Listen first, decide second

On the call or in the email thread:

- Let the guest tell the story. Do not interrupt with a defense.
- Reflect back what you heard. ("Just so I have it right — you waited
  35 minutes, then felt rushed when you asked about swelling. Did I miss
  anything?")
- Ask one open question: *"What would feel like a fair resolution to you?"*
- Take notes you can paste into the internal record.

### B3. Pick a resolution

Common, defensible resolutions for med spas:

- A complimentary follow-up consult with a senior provider
- A partial credit or refund of the affected service (not a blanket "we'll
  refund everything" — anchor it to the specific issue)
- A pause and reassessment of a treatment plan
- An aftercare check-in call from the provider directly
- An apology with no compensation (yes, this is sometimes the right answer)

Document who approved which resolution. Owners should approve any refund
above a pre-set threshold (e.g. $200).

### B4. Close the loop publicly — only if the guest asks

If — and only if — the guest later updates or asks you to update the public
review, reply again briefly:

> Thank you for circling back, [guest first name]. We appreciate the chance
> to make this right and look forward to seeing you again.

Never edit the original public reply to remove the apology. Visible repair
is part of the trust signal for future readers.

## Section C — Internal tracker (one row per low-rated review)

Log every 1- or 2-star review even if the guest never replies privately.
This becomes your operational signal over time.

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Date posted | Public review date |
| Source | Google / Yelp / Facebook / other |
| Star rating | 1 or 2 |
| Guest name (if visible) | Public name only |
| Reviewed visit date | If identifiable |
| Provider on record | Internal only — never in public reply |
| Public reply posted by | Name + timestamp |
| Public reply approved by | Second-reviewer name |
| Private outreach attempted | Date + channel |
| Private outreach response | Yes / No / Pending |
| Resolution | Refund / credit / consult / apology / no action |
| Approver of resolution | Name |
| Outcome | Public update / private close-out / no response |
| Lesson for the team | One sentence |

The Google Sheet version of this tracker ships in the purchase toolkit.

## Section D — When to escalate above the practice manager

Loop in the owner and (where applicable) counsel when any of the following
appear in a review:

- An allegation of provider misconduct, harm, or negligence
- A regulatory or licensing reference
- Anything that reads like a draft of a complaint to the state board
- A demand for a refund tied to a clinical outcome claim
- Repeat low-rated reviews from the same guest across platforms

In these cases, **do not post any public reply** until the owner and
counsel have reviewed. Acknowledge internally; respond externally only
with approved language.

---

## Tone reminders before you post

- Calm beats clever. Professional beats defensive. Short beats thorough.
- The public reply is for *future* guests reading the review later. They
  are the actual audience.
- If you wouldn't say it on stage at a conference, don't post it.
